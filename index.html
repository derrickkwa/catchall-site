The context layer · being built

Everything a decision rests on,
gathered in one place.

Today it is scattered across tools, or held in someone’s head. The decision layer weighs every input for trust, strength, age and reach, and flags what’s missing, instead of stuffing it all into a prompt.

Performance data
Every platform the work runs on, reconciled to one grain instead of one dashboard each.
Past learnings
What was decided and why, what it weighed, what was predicted, and what actually happened.
Retrieval and scoping
Once you know what is wrong, current retrieval finds the history that bears on it. The harder step is the one before: choosing which of thousands of metrics deserves a look today, when nothing has flagged yet.

How does it actually work?

Every decision makes the next one smarter.

Every call is predicted before anything runs, checked against the outcome and against doing nothing, then written back so the next call starts smarter.

Better models make this loop stronger. No model can create the record it depends on.

Why can’t AI do it today?

Judgment is a math problem,
not a prompt.

Prediction works where the world repeats: one huge shared dataset, outcomes you can check every day. A company has data nobody else has, results that arrive late and tangled, and a new competitor, rule or stockout every quarter. No global dataset can run it.

Statistical

Cause

Which call actually moved the number? Results arrive late, and tangled with everything else.

Our approach Experiments over correlation

Holdouts and deliberate variation inside partner businesses, so the engine learns what a decision caused rather than what moved alongside it: the confounder nobody logged, two levers that always move together, the tide.

Mathematical

Confidence

How sure should it be? A call is only worth money if its confidence is honest.

Our approach Calibrated confidence

Every call carries a probability, and training rewards how honest it proves to be, using a proper scoring rule. A stated 70% should come true about seven times in ten. Models trained on pass/fail learn to sound certain instead.

Technological

Record

What would have happened otherwise? That data doesn’t exist. It has to be made, call by call.

Our approach Local, informed by the network

Each business borrows from what every other business has taught the model, then leans on its own evidence as it builds up. What worked for Coca-Cola may not work for a local craft cola.

Three more questions we’re researching
  • Training on outcomes. Which mix of expert examples, outcome-verified rewards and calibration rewards builds judgment fastest, and how much of the gain comes from the data rather than the method.
  • Statistics against language models. Whether a language model with full context beats a plain statistical model on the same questions, and where each belongs.
  • Value of information. When another week of data would change the call, and when it is time to commit.

Today’s AI is trained to sound right. Judgment has to be graded on being right.
