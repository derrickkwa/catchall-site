Applied AI research · 2026

Work is being automated. Judgment isn’t.

The AI decision layer trained on real feedback.

AI today makes a formidable intern. What’s missing is everything an executive does: deciding what matters, what to do about it, and whether it worked.

01 · The gap

Two kinds of AI work are funded. The one between them is underbuilt.

Funded

Applied AI

Takes existing models and deploys them into existing businesses. Apps, forward-deployed engineers, agents as a service.

Underbuilt

Judgment

The models, methods and priors a machine needs to decide well inside a particular field. Neither side has made it their problem.

Funded

AI research

Extends what those models can do. Continual learning, world models, new attention mechanisms.

Models only recently got good enough at execution that deciding became the constraint. Model providers now sell decisions as an API: a fast, typed answer about the input in front of the model. That is classification. A business decision is a bet on what happens next. Decision-intelligence software already recommends what to do, but it rarely commits to a forecast before the outcome or gets scored against doing nothing, so there is no record of whether its judgment is any good. That record is what we are building.

02 · The problem

A general model brought to a specialist decision has no priors but the internet’s.

Judgment needs more than reasoning. It draws on a field’s accumulated frameworks, the things it has already established, the methods that actually measure it, and a record of what has worked and what hasn’t. Take those away and a very capable model will reason fluently to an average answer, and sound certain doing it.

That body of work doesn’t exist for marketing, and it doesn’t exist for most fields. As long as a person owns the decision, the reasoning behind it rarely leaves their head. Nothing is written down, so nothing is scored, so the next decision starts where the last one did.

Nothing compounds.

03 · The engine

We are building a judgment engine.

One half assembles what a decision rests on. The other is the research into what should be making the decision.

Being built

The context layer

Everything a decision in the field rests on, gathered in one place. Today it is scattered across tools, or held in someone’s head.

Performance data
Every platform the work runs on, reconciled to one grain instead of one dashboard each.
Past learnings
What was decided and why, what it weighed, what was predicted, and what actually happened.
Retrieval and scoping
Once you know what is wrong, current retrieval finds the history that bears on it. The open problem is the step before: choosing which of thousands of metrics deserves a look today, when nothing has flagged yet.
Three more sources it draws on
  • Frameworks. What the field has already established, loaded as priors and revised when the evidence disagrees.
  • Category context. Where demand is, who else is competing for it, and which way the market is moving.
  • Company context. Goals, constraints and economics: margin, inventory, cash, and what this effort is for.

Point a frontier model at this and it already beats the same model working from scattered tools. That is the floor the research builds on.

The open problem

The research

How a model learns to make the call, and to say how sure it is. The training signal is real outcomes, graded against what would have happened anyway.

Calibrated confidence
Every call carries a probability, and training rewards how honest that probability proves to be, using a proper scoring rule. A stated 70% should come true about seven times in ten. Models trained on pass/fail learn to sound certain instead.
Experiments over correlation
Holdouts and deliberate variation inside partner businesses, so the engine learns what a decision caused rather than what moved alongside it: the confounder nobody logged, two levers that always move together, the tide.
Local, informed by the network
Each business borrows from what every other business has taught the model, then leans on its own evidence as it builds up. What worked for Coca-Cola may not work for a local craft cola.
Three more questions we are researching
  • Training on outcomes. Which mix of expert examples, outcome-verified rewards and calibration rewards builds judgment fastest, and how much of the gain comes from the data rather than the method.
  • Statistics against language models. Whether a language model with full context beats a plain statistical model on the same questions, and where each belongs.
  • Value of information. When another week of data would change the call, and when it is time to commit.

We think the step change comes from what the model learns from, rather than from a larger general model.

05 · Fit

No two businesses are the same problem.

Which frameworks apply, what data exists, which methods identify anything at all: these change from one business to the next. Ship a single fixed model and you have described an average company that doesn’t exist.

So the structure is shared and the fit is not. The record, the machinery and the method library are common to every business. Which methods apply, how far each one is trusted, and where the thresholds sit are fitted per business and re-calibrated as evidence arrives, by the system, not by hand.

06 · Why we can do this

Our partner businesses are an instrumented lab.

Every decision is logged before it is made and tested against what actually happened. Most judgment systems learn from whatever happened to be recorded. Ours can learn from experiments it designs.

The standing to experiment

We run the accounts, so we can require an experiment rather than ask for one.

A vendor can’t hold out part of a client’s spend to measure what would have happened anyway. Inside our partner businesses, a holdout is a decision we are allowed to make.

07 · Trajectory

The end state is judgment across every decision a business makes.

Each function needs its own context and builds its own record. The machinery is the same; what it learns is not.

We start in marketing.

Marketing judgment is opinionated, constantly exercised, and it settles against numbers on a timescale you can learn from.

It is also honest about the difficulty. Marketing outcomes are noisy, lagged and confounded.

If the method holds here, it holds in quieter places.

Marketing
Where we are building now.
Operations
Its own context, its own record.
Finance
Same machinery, different evidence.
Inventory & ERP
Demand, stock and lead times, settled against what sold.
